# 02 — Core Loop and Progression

## Core loop

1. Discover an opportunity through a contract, scientific question, market need, or strategic goal.
2. Research the environment and plan a mission.
3. Design a craft from available parts and reusable assemblies.
4. Validate, finance, build, crew when applicable, and launch it.
5. Directly fly the demanding phases and use planning/automation where capability allows.
6. Gather data, deliver payload/value, and recover people/equipment where applicable.
7. Convert results into funds, scientific understanding, access, reputation, and infrastructure.
8. Reinvest to attempt missions that were previously impossible or uneconomic.

## Progression currencies

The proposal separates several forms of progress instead of using one universal point pool:

- **Money:** buys labor, materials, facilities, launches, maintenance, and services.
- **Scientific data/understanding:** evidence tied to phenomena, instruments, environments, and experiment quality.
- **Engineering experience:** potentially reduces uncertainty, unlocks refinements, or improves company procedures; exact form is open.
- **Access/trust:** contracts, licenses, launch sites, markets, partnerships, and political permissions.
- **Infrastructure:** persistent capacity that changes mission cost, range, risk, and cadence.

Not all currencies must appear in the first playable. Funds plus scientific data are the minimum proposed pair.

## Capability eras

Eras describe capability, not mandatory calendar locks:

1. **Commercial launch:** chemical propulsion, suborbital/orbital missions, basic satellites and science.
2. **Cislunar operations:** reliable reuse, docking, depots, crewed operations, sustained orbital infrastructure.
3. **Inner-system industry:** asteroid/lunar mining, manufacturing, shipyards, regular interplanetary logistics.
4. **System settlement:** habitats and colonies, stronger corporations, local politics, outer-planet operations.
5. **High-energy expansion:** fusion and advanced propulsion, industrialized outer system, large mobile assets.
6. **Antimatter/speculative future:** expensive transformative capabilities and optional late-game projects.

Early-rocketry starts may later prepend historical eras. They should reuse the same rules where possible rather than become a separate game mode.

## Sandbox and optional endings

Campaigns are open-ended. Optional finite goals can provide a sense of completion—scientific, economic, infrastructural, or political—without forcing retirement. A completed objective unlocks an epilogue/recognition and continued play.

## Failure and recovery

Individual missions can fail. The company should usually have a recovery path through smaller contracts, insurance/investment if selected, asset liquidation, or difficulty assists. Whether hard bankruptcy or irreversible campaign failure exists is open.

## Decisions

| Decision | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Sandbox campaigns with optional finite goals and continued play | Confirmed | User response |
| Funds and scientific data anchor initial progression | Proposed | Directly connects company and exploration loops |
| Automation grows with demonstrated capability | Proposed | Supports transition into strategic command without erasing flight |
| Hard bankruptcy/permadeath | Open | Major effect on long-campaign tone and difficulty |
